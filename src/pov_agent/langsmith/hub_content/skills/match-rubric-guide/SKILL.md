# Match rubric guide

Every review uses this exact structure. The structure evaluator (offline and online)
checks for these section headings, so follow them precisely.

## Structure (use these `##` headings, in order)

1. `# <Candidate> for <Role>` — a specific H1 naming the candidate and the role.
2. `## Match Summary` — 3–5 sentences. Lead with the overall verdict and the match
   score. Written last, after strengths and gaps exist. Include the score on its own
   line: `Match score: 0.NN`.
3. `## Strengths` — where the candidate meets or exceeds requirements. Each bullet ties
   to a specific job requirement with direct evidence from the resume.
4. `## Gaps` — required qualifications or responsibilities the resume does not evidence.
   Be concrete about what is missing; distinguish a hard miss from a partial match.
5. `## Recommendation` — a clear next step: **Advance**, **Hold**, or **Decline**, with
   one or two sentences of justification tied to the gaps. No hedging.

## Scoring (0.0–1.0)

- **0.85–1.0** — meets every required qualification with direct evidence; most
  nice-to-haves too. Advance.
- **0.6–0.84** — meets the core requirements with some partial matches or one notable
  gap. Advance or Hold depending on the gap.
- **0.35–0.59** — misses one or more core requirements; only transferable adjacency.
  Usually Hold or Decline.
- **0.0–0.34** — misses most core requirements; wrong-domain or under-leveled. Decline.

Anchor the score to evidence, not tone. A polished resume that misses the requirements
still scores low; a plain resume that meets them scores high.

## Fairness rules (non-negotiable)

- Judge the qualifications, never the person. A candidate's **name, gender, pronouns,
  age, national origin, race, ethnicity, religion, disability, marital/family status, or
  school prestige** must never affect the score or appear as a factor in the reasoning.
- If the resume states a protected attribute, ignore it. Two resumes identical except for
  such an attribute must receive the same score and the same recommendation.
- Credit genuine transferable skills; never use demographic proxies such as "culture fit",
  "school pedigree", or name-based assumptions about background.

## Voice

- Direct and concrete. Cite the requirement and the evidence together.
- Show your reasoning when you reach the score.
- Never invent experience, employers, or credentials the resume does not state.
